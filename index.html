Who bakes it

It's one person, one oven,
and one bake a week.

I started making milk bread because I couldn't find it near me. The first dozen batches were dense enough to prop a door open. Somewhere around the thirtieth I stopped trying to rush the cold rise, and it finally came out the way I remembered it.

Everything is mixed by hand in my kitchen on Friday night and baked on Saturday morning. I only make what's been ordered, which is why the list closes Wednesday, and why there's never a tray sitting out going stale.

The method

What a cooked-flour starter does to a crumb

Tangzhong is the whole trick. You cook a little of the flour into the milk first, which lets the dough hold far more water than it otherwise could.

0%
None. Straight dough. Too much
  • Water the dough will hold 62%
  • Still soft on day two 30%
  • Holds its rise 100%

For 500g of flour

Starter
nothing to cook
Then add
500g flour, 310g milk

Cook the starter to 65°C, until a spoon drawn through it leaves a line.

Tight, close crumb, and dry by tomorrow.

  • Cooked starter Roughly a twentieth of the flour, cooked into the milk before anything else is mixed.
  • Overnight cold rise The dough rests in the fridge until Saturday. Slower fermentation, more flavour, no extra sugar.
  • Baked to the list Nothing sits in a case. The box you collect came out of the oven about two hours earlier.

      Questions

      How far ahead do I order?
      Wednesday night for that Saturday, and payment has to be in by then too. Later than that, send it anyway. Sometimes there's room, and you'll get an honest answer if there isn't.
      How do I pay?
      Cash App, Zelle or Apple Cash, up front. Apple Cash is sent from Messages, the other two from their own apps. The handles are all on your ticket once you've built a box. Put your name and pickup date in the payment note so it can be matched to your order. Nothing is charged on this website and no card details are ever collected.
      Why pay in advance?
      Because everything is made to order in one bake. The shopping and the mixing happen against the paid list, so an unpaid box is one that doesn't get made, and paying is what stops someone else taking the slot.
      What if I need to cancel?
      Tell me before Wednesday's cutoff and you get a full refund, same way you paid. After the cutoff the ingredients are bought and the dough is planned, so it can't be refunded, but the box is yours, and it can usually be held for a later Saturday or passed to someone else if you'd rather.
      How do I keep them?
      Sealed at room temperature, best for two days. Don't refrigerate, it dries them out fast. Ten seconds in the microwave or five minutes in a warm oven brings one back.
      Can I freeze them?
      Yes, and they take it well. Bag them the day you get them, thaw at room temperature, warm briefly. Custard-filled are the exception: eat those fresh.
      What's in them?
      Flour, milk, butter, eggs, sugar, salt, yeast, plus whatever the flavor adds. Made in a kitchen that handles wheat, dairy, eggs, soy, sesame and tree nuts, so no batch can be promised free of them. Put allergies in the notes and you'll get a straight answer about what's possible.
      Do you deliver?
      Pickup only for now. Three boxes or more and it's worth asking. If you're close by, something can usually be worked out.
      Bigger orders and events?
      Happily. Two weeks' notice for anything over ten boxes. Send the form with the numbers in the notes.

      Bringing them back

      They are best the morning you collect them. After that, thirty seconds of effort gets you most of the way back.

      10 seconds

      Lay a damp piece of kitchen paper over the top first. It stops the crumb drying while the steam does the work. Longer than fifteen seconds and it goes tough.

      5 minutes at 300°F / 150°C

      The best result if you have the patience. The outside firms up again and the inside goes soft. Straight on the rack, no foil.

      3 minutes at 300°F / 150°C

      Quick, and very good on the cinnamon sugar ones. Watch the custard-filled: the filling gets hotter than the bread looks.

      Thaw first, then any of the above

      Out of the bag and on the counter for an hour. Don't reheat from frozen: the outside dries out before the middle wakes up.
