Why it's different

Three things make a Hokkaido bite

Milk bread isn't a recipe so much as a technique. These are the parts we don't cut corners on.

Tangzhong

A spoonful of flour cooked into milk before anything else. It locks moisture into the dough, which is why these stay soft on day two instead of going stale by dinner.

A long, cold rise

The dough rests overnight in the fridge. Slower fermentation means more flavor in the crumb — a faint sweetness that doesn't come from adding more sugar.

Baked to order

Nothing sits in a case. We bake against Saturday's order list, so the box you carry home came out of the oven a couple of hours earlier.

Good to know

Questions

How far ahead do I need to order?

Wednesday night for that Saturday. If you're later than that, send the form anyway — sometimes there's room, and we'll tell you honestly if there isn't.

How do I keep them?

Sealed at room temperature, they're best for two days. Don't refrigerate — it dries them out fast. To bring one back, ten seconds in the microwave or five minutes in a warm oven.

Can you freeze them?

Yes, and they take it well. Freeze in a bag the day you get them, then thaw at room temperature and warm briefly. Custard-filled ones are the exception — eat those fresh.

What's in them?

Flour, milk, butter, eggs, sugar, salt, yeast, plus whatever the flavor adds. Everything is made in a kitchen that handles wheat, dairy, eggs, soy, sesame, and tree nuts, so we can't promise a batch is free of any of those. Tell us about allergies in the notes and we'll be straight with you about what we can do.

Do you deliver?

Pickup only for now. Orders of three boxes or more, ask — if you're close by, we can usually work something out.

Bigger orders and events?

Happy to. Two weeks' notice for anything over ten boxes. Send the form with the numbers in the notes and we'll go from there.
