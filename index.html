The baker

Marcus Vela

Marcus started as a line cook who kept getting sent to the pastry station because he was the only one with the patience for it. Eighteen years later, that's the whole job.

He trained under a pastry chef who made him pipe the same border for six weeks before letting him near a finished cake — a cruelty he now describes as the single most useful thing anyone ever did for him. He spent nine years in hotel kitchens and restaurant pastry departments, ran the dessert program at a place that no longer exists, and left to open Chaos Confections out of a kitchen barely big enough to turn around in.

The name is a joke that stopped being one. Every good bake starts as a mess — flour everywhere, four things happening at once, a timer going off somewhere. The craft isn't avoiding the chaos. It's knowing exactly where it's headed.

“You can teach technique in an afternoon. Patience takes a bit longer.”

These days Marcus splits his week between orders and lessons, and he still finishes every cake himself. If you order something, he made it. That's the entire business model.
